Layout

Three classes to place the content on the page: a centered container, a gallery that spreads cards in columns and a two-column grid. The last two use CSS Grid and adapt by themselves to the width of the screen.

Container

ez-container centers the content and limits its width on large screens, so that the lines of text are not too long. On small ones it leaves a margin on each side. It usually wraps all the content of the page, under the navigation bar.

This very page is inside an ez-container: make the browser window narrower to see how it adjusts.

Gallery

ez-gallery spreads its children in as many columns as fit, at least 17rem wide each, and the ones left over go to the next row. You do not say how many columns: with a wide window there are three or four, and on a phone, one. It is useful for a photo gallery, a list of cards or anything else.

The rule is grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fit, minmax(17rem, 1fr)): repeat as many columns as fit (auto-fit), each one between 17rem and a share of the free space (1fr).

Two columns

ez-split puts its children in columns: by default, two, and the first one is twice as wide as the second one (2fr 1fr). On a narrow screen they become a single column, one under the other. It is useful for a main content with secondary information next to it.

Another split

The --ez-split-columns variable changes the split of a single element, with the style attribute: two equal columns (1fr 1fr), the narrow one on the left (1fr 2fr) or three columns (1fr 1fr 1fr). It accepts any value of grid-template-columns.

Page with a sidebar

A page with the navigation bar on the left is another use of the two columns: ez-split on the <body>, with a narrow first column. The bar has the ez-navbar ez-navbar--side variant (see Components), and the content, its ez-container: ez-split splits the page in columns, and ez-container centers the content inside its own.

On a narrow screen, the bar goes to the top, as in any ez-split.
